# US nickel supply chain — geography

Research pass 2026-09-08, companion to
[nickel-refining-siting-2026-09.md](nickel-refining-siting-2026-09.md). Michigan
and Maine first, then the rest of the country. Coordinates marked UNVERIFIED

## 1. The finding that reframes the whole exercise

**Upper Michigan nickel is currently smelted in Canada.** Eagle Mine ore is
trucked ~105 km to Humboldt Mill, milled to concentrate, and railed out over a
CN spur to a smelter in **Sudbury, Ontario**
([Bridge Michigan](https://bridgemi.com/michigan-environment-watch/mining-back-michigans-upper-peninsula-heres-how-it-works/);
[Fluor](https://www.fluor.com/projects/lundin-mining-eagle-mine)). The only
primary nickel mine in the United States has no domestic smelter to sell to.

Talon Metals completed the acquisition of Eagle Mine and Humboldt Mill from
Lundin Mining in January 2026, creating a pure-play American nickel-copper
company; Lundin kept ~19.9% of Talon
([Talon](https://talonmetals.com/talon-metals-completes-previously-announced-acquisition-of-lundin-minings-eagle-mine-and-humboldt-mill-operations-creating-a-multi-asset-u-s-nickel-copper-company/)).
Eagle has produced >194,000 t nickel since 2013.

## 2. Maine's mining law does not apply to a refinery

Maine's LD 820 and DEP **Chapter 200** are routinely described as the strictest
state metal-mining rules in the country — open-pit ban, wet tailings
impoundment ban, strict groundwater limits
([Maine Legislature](https://legislature.maine.gov/legis/bills/bills_128th/billtexts/SP026501.asp);
[NRCM](https://www.nrcm.org/programs/waters/metal-mining-pollution-maine/maine-mining-law/)).

Chapter 200's scope **excludes smelting and refining**. DEP's own legislative
testimony is that "smelting and refining are fundamentally different types of
activities, more akin to those taking place at a manufacturing facility,"
already covered by other federal and state law, and that pulling them into
Chapter 200 would be redundant
([DEP testimony](https://legislature.maine.gov/legis/bills/getTestimonyDoc.asp?id=186510)).

So a Maine refinery running concentrate or MHP shipped in from elsewhere goes
through ordinary Site Location of Development Act review plus standard air and
water permitting — a materially lower bar than in-state mining faces. The
political risk does not vanish: organized opposition to the Warren/Union
exploration (the CARMA group, a change.org petition) shows Maine will mobilize
against metals projects, and a refinery could attract that by association
([Bangor Daily News](https://www.bangordailynews.com/2023/02/27/midcoast/exiro-mining-warren-union/)).

The Warren/Union deposit itself (Exiro Minerals, around Crawford Pond,
44.191, −69.245) has no published grade or tonnage and shows no material
update since 2023. Treat it as stalled.

## 3. Michigan

**Marquette County is the live cluster.** Beyond Eagle and Humboldt:

* **REV Nickel** — Revex Technologies with Eagle Mine, reprocessing mine
  tailings and spent lithium-ion batteries into nickel concentrate. **$145M
  DOE grant** signed January 2025 plus **$50M** from Michigan's Make It in
  Michigan Competitiveness Fund against a $398M project cost, targeting enough
  nickel for ~500,000 EV batteries a year
  ([Upper Michigan's Source](https://www.uppermichiganssource.com/2025/03/10/revex-technologies-receives-145m-grant-production-critical-minerals/)).
  Michigan has already put state money into nickel processing.
* **Empire Mine** — Cleveland-Cliffs iron ore mine and pellet plant, idled
  since 2016. Large processing footprint with rail and power already there.
  Coordinates and owner intent UNVERIFIED.
* **White Pine, Ontonagon County** — copper smelter and mine, 1952–1995,
  >4 billion lb copper. Now Kinterra Copper USA's "White Pine North" copper
  redevelopment, not nickel
  ([Mining.com](https://www.mining.com/highland-copper-exits-white-pine-north-project-for-30m/)).
* **Escanaba** — Billerud's mill is **active** with ~860 employees, not a
  brownfield. Rule it out.

Permitting: Part 632 of NREPA governs nonferrous *mining* and Eagle Mine is its
only operating permit. A standalone refinery processing feedstock trucked or
railed in would instead go through EGLE's ordinary Permit to Install / New
Source Review under Rule 201 plus Title V. UP nonattainment status by pollutant
was not confirmed county by county.

## 4. Maine's mill sites

The paper industry left behind exactly the asset a refinery wants — large
industrial parcels with water rights, hydro, and rail — but most are already
taken or power-constrained.

| Site | Status |
|---|---|
| **One North**, Millinocket / E. Millinocket (fmr. Great Northern Paper) | 1,400 acres, vacant since 2008, up to 126 MW hydro nameplate, rail, Opportunity Zone. **A $300M data center here was cancelled in 2025 because the site could not deliver enough power** ([BDN](https://www.bangordailynews.com/2025/04/09/mainefocus/mainefocus-business/300m-data-center-former-millinocket-paper-mill-canceled/)) — nameplate hydro is not deliverable load. |
| **Androscoggin Mill**, Jay (fmr. Pixelle) | Closed 2023, bought Jan 2024 by JGT2 Redevelopment **including the adjacent cogeneration plant**, marketed for industrial reuse. Best available Maine candidate. |
| **Bucksport** (fmr. Verso) | 123-acre core in a 250-acre waterfront site, AIM-owned; ~104 ac sold to Whole Oceans. On-site **161 MW** plant sold separately. A March 2026 pier-modification request hints at new plans. |
| **Lincoln** | Town-owned ~400 ac; Form Energy and Biofine already tenants. Limited remaining room. |
| Old Town, Baileyville, Skowhegan, Madison | **Operating or re-occupied.** Not candidates. |

Ports: **Searsport / Mack Point** is the deep-water cargo terminal — Port
Authority dry-cargo dock at 800' LOA / 120' beam / 39' draft.

**Northern Maine is electrically isolated.** NMISA covers Aroostook and
Washington counties, ~54,000 customers, with ties only to New Brunswick — not
part of ISO-NE or NEPOOL ([NMISA](https://www.nmisa.com/)). The mill sites above
sit in CMP/ISO-NE territory rather than NMISA, but Millinocket's cancelled data
center shows ISO-NE-connected Maine sites still hit deliverability limits.

## 5. Honest read on MI and ME

Both states are **500–900 miles from the demand they would serve**. The
stainless and battery cluster is Ohio–Kentucky–Tennessee–Georgia: North
American Stainless at Ghent KY, Ultium at Warren OH and Spring Hill TN,
BlueOval SK at Glendale KY, the SK and Hyundai plants in Georgia. That distance
is precisely what Electra is designing around by choosing the Southeast.

Michigan's case is feedstock: it has the ore, the trades, Michigan Tech's
metallurgy pipeline, a state government that has already co-funded nickel
processing, and an operator publicly weighing a Michigan processing site
against North Dakota. Maine's case is the permitting carve-out plus mill sites
with water and deep-water port access — against demonstrably constrained power.

## 6. The strongest reuse candidate in the country is in Louisiana

**Port Nickel, Braithwaite, Plaquemines Parish (29.858, −89.963)** — built
around 1955 by Freeport Sulphur to refine nickel-cobalt ore from Moa Bay, Cuba;
kept running after Castro nationalized the mine and operated as **AMAX Metals
Recovery, a nickel-cobalt refinery, from 1958 until 2000**
([EPA ready-for-reuse determination](https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2025-05/amax-metals-ready-for-reuse-determination.pdf)).

The 387-acre core tract is **currently for sale ($29.9M) or lease**, listed as
of 2026-07-19, with a 510' × 52' deep-water Mississippi River dock, Norfolk
Southern Class I rail on an active on-site spur with reciprocal switching to
KCS, CN, UP and BNSF/CSX, Foreign Trade Zone designation, and Entergy service
([CBRE via Louisiana Site Selection](https://louisianasiteselection.com/led/Property/Detail/22215/Port-Nickel-Mississippi-Riverfront-Heavy-Industrial-Tract-w-)).

It is a former nickel refinery, on deep water, with Class I rail, in the Gulf
Coast sulfuric-acid supply region, for sale today. Nothing else found in this
pass combines those.

## 7. Expansion order after MI and ME

1. **Oklahoma** — Westwin Elements, Lawton. The most advanced dedicated US
   nickel refinery. Phase 1 targets 18,000 t/yr by 2028. Rail-fed, landlocked.
2. **Minnesota** — Talon's Tamarack, the most advanced US nickel sulfide
   deposit in permitting; EIS scoping comment period ran Jul–Sep 2026.
3. **North Dakota** — Talon's Beulah facility, $114.8M DOE, on a former
   Westmoreland coal mine. The clearest legacy-industrial reuse precedent.
4. **Southeast (AL/GA/SC)** — Electra's stated target. Deep-water ports, the
   battery corridor, and the black-mass recyclers already there.
5. **Missouri** — Madison Mine, Fredericktown, 1,800 acres, operating Co-Ni-Cu
   with a hydromet facility near completion.
6. **Louisiana / Gulf Coast** — Port Nickel plus the sulfuric acid supply.
7. **Montana** — Sibanye's Columbus complex is a real operating metallurgical
   smelter handling nickel-bearing material, but PGM-primary and financially
   stressed.
8. **Alaska** — Ambler district is Cu-Co-primary and access-constrained. Last.

**Sulfuric acid** in bulk comes from the Florida and Louisiana
phosphate-fertilizer complexes plus byproduct acid at the surviving copper
smelters (Hayden AZ, Miami AZ, Garfield UT)
([USGS Sulfur MCS 2025](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2025/mcs2025-sulfur.pdf)).

## 8. Other US nonferrous sites, and why most are not available

Hayden AZ (ASARCO) is closed and was in reopening talks as of 2025. Miami AZ
and Garfield UT are the two operating US copper smelters. **El Paso TX**
(ASARCO, closed 1999) sits in an environmental custodial trust with
institutional controls limiting it to commercial reuse after lead, arsenic,
cadmium and chromium cleanup — a weak candidate despite the pedigree
([TCEQ](https://www.tceq.texas.gov/remediation/sites/asarco)).
